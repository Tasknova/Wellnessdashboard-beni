## 1. Loyalty Programs

### Apollo — OneApollo Program
- **Card-less** loyalty spanning all Apollo entities (Hospitals, Pharmacy, Diagnostics, Clinics)
- **Tiers:** Silver (free, min INR 500 spend), Gold (INR 75K annual), Platinum (premium)
- **Health Credits:** Silver 10%, Gold 10–15%, Platinum 10–20% of bill value
- **Redemption:** 1 HC = INR 1, redeemable at Apollo stores; online max 50% of order

### Apollo 24/7 Circle Membership
- INR 99/2 months, INR 199/6 months, INR 299/12 months
- Benefits: Free delivery (orders > INR 199), priority doctor access (15 min), 60% off diagnostics, accidental insurance (INR 1 L from Niva Bupa)

### MedPlus — MedPlus Wallet
- Prepaid wallet (min INR 3K, max INR 20K balance)
- 20% of loaded amount = Flexicash (additional cashback on select items)
- Regular 20% discount on medicines
- Scale: 10M+ loyal customers

### Wellness Forever — Wellness Chakra
- INR 99 for 6 months
- Additional 5% discount on pharma products (over in-store discounts)
- Up to 30 purchases/month

### PharmEasy Plus
- Extra 5% cashback on medicines, 10% on diagnostics
- Free delivery, free teleconsultations
- Often offered free via bank partnerships

### What Drives Repeat Purchases
- Chronic medication needs (non-negotiable)
- Price consistency and discounting (20–40% is table stakes)
- Convenience (delivery speed, availability)
- Ecosystem lock-in (health records, Rx history, lab results on one platform)

## 2. Customer Segmentation — RFM in Pharmacy

### Pharmacy-Specific Segments

| Segment | Profile | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Chronic patients** | Diabetes, hypertension, thyroid — monthly refills | 60–70% of repeat orders |
| **Acute/Episodic** | Antibiotics, pain relief — unpredictable, low frequency | Short relationship |
| **Wellness/Preventive** | Vitamins, supplements — discretionary, growing | Higher margins |
| **Beauty/Personal care** | Skincare, haircare at pharmacy chains | Lifestyle-driven |
| **OTC self-medicators** | Antacids, cough syrup without Rx | Convenience-driven, price-sensitive |

### RFM Framework for Pharmacy

| Segment | Recency | Frequency | Monetary | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Champions | Very recent | Monthly+ | High | Reward, cross-sell devices/diagnostics |
| Loyal | Recent | Monthly | Medium-High | Maintain, upsell supplements |
| At Risk | 60–90 day gap | Was high | Medium | Win-back: call + offer |
| Hibernating | 90+ days | Low | Low | Reactivation campaign |
| New/Promising | Very recent | 1–2 purchases | Variable | Nurture to second purchase |

India has **77M+ diabetics** and **75M targeted** for hypertension/diabetes standard care — these chronic patients are the primary value segment.

## 3. Churn & Retention

### Benchmarks
- **Tata 1mg:** 70% retention rate (30% annual churn)
- Chronic patients have significantly lower churn than acute buyers
- Acquiring new customer costs **5–7x more** than retaining existing

### Why Customers Leave
1. **Price:** Customers switch for even 5% better pricing (extreme price sensitivity)
2. **Stockouts:** Out-of-stock on chronic medication = immediate churn trigger
3. **Delivery delays:** Beyond 24–48 hours drives switching for online
4. **Generic switching:** Truemeds grew to INR 325 Cr revenue by offering 30–80% savings

### Retention Impact
- Increasing retention by **5% increases profits by 25–95%** (general retail benchmark)

## 5. Prescription Refill Cycles

### Typical Intervals

| Condition | Common Meds | Refill Cycle |
|---|---|---|
| Diabetes (Type 2) | Metformin, Glimepiride | Monthly |
| Hypertension | Amlodipine, Telmisartan | Monthly |
| Thyroid | Levothyroxine | Monthly–Quarterly |
| Cardiovascular | Statins, Aspirin | Monthly |
| Asthma/COPD | Inhalers, Montelukast | Monthly |

### Adherence Rates in India
- **WHO benchmark:** ~50% of patients don't take meds as prescribed
- Diabetes adherence: **43.4%** (meta-analysis)
- Hypertension: 19–96% range (wide variation)
- 40% of enrollees showed low refill adherence (medication gap ≥ 20%)

### Refill Reminder Channels
- **WhatsApp:** Most effective in India — send 3–5 days before refill due date
- Automated call reminders: patients refilled more (30.3% vs 24.9%)
- App push notifications: all major e-pharmacies use
- IVR calls: effective for elderly patients

## 6. Personalization & Cross-Selling

### Cross-Sell Opportunities
- **Rx → Supplements:** Statins + CoQ10; Metformin + Vitamin B12; Hypertension + Magnesium
- **Rx → Devices:** Diabetics + glucometers/test strips; Hypertension + BP monitors
- **Rx → Diagnostics:** HbA1c for diabetics; lipid panels for CVD patients
- **Seasonal bundles:** Cold medicines + vitamins during flu season

### Personalization Strategies
- Medication suggestions based on Rx history
- Disease management programs (Apollo Sugar for diabetes)
- Targeted promotions by therapeutic area

## 8. Patient vs Customer — Pharmacy CRM Differences

| Dimension | Regular Retail | Pharmacy |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase necessity | Discretionary | Often non-negotiable |
| Data sensitivity | Preferences | Health conditions, prescriptions |
| Regulatory burden | Consumer laws | DPDP Act + Pharmacy Act + Drug Schedules |
| Purchase frequency | Varies | Predictable cycles (chronic) |
| Cross-sell ethics | Few constraints | Must be therapeutically appropriate |
| Switching cost | Low | Medium-high (Rx history, trust) |

### Health Data Privacy (DPDP Act 2023)
- Explicit informed consent mandatory
- Penalties: up to **INR 250 Cr** per breach
- Medical records = sensitive personal data

### Prescription Data as Competitive Moat
- Creates switching costs (patients don't want to re-upload history)
- Enables: auto-refill, drug interaction checks, personalized recommendations
- Apollo 24/7, Tata 1mg, PharmEasy all build comprehensive health profiles

## Key Takeaways

1. **Chronic patients = backbone** — 60–70% of repeat orders, CLV of INR 1.2L–12L
2. **Adherence is the opportunity** — Only 43% of Indian diabetics are adherent; WhatsApp reminders 3–5 days before refill are most effective
3. **Retention beats acquisition 5–7x** — Tata 1mg proved it by cutting ad spend 37% while growing revenue
4. **Subscription models converging** — Every major player has a membership tier (INR 99–299/year)
5. **Generic switching is disruptive** — Truemeds doubled revenue; Jan Aushadhi saw 177x sales growth
6. **Prescription data is the moat** — Health records + Rx history create switching costs beyond price
7. **Pharmacy CRM ≠ retail CRM** — Non-discretionary needs, predictable cycles, ethical/regulatory constraints
