- Mobile orders: **73%** of e-pharmacy orders via apps (2025)
- OTC products: **56%** of online pharmacy sales; 44% prescription

## 2. Online vs Offline Dynamics

### Channel Split (2024)
- Brick-and-mortar: **~75%** of pharmacy sales
- Online: **~25%** and growing at 16–17% CAGR
- User penetration: 7.54% (2025), expected 10% by 2029

### Category Performance by Channel
- **OTC, supplements, wellness:** Sell better online (56% of e-pharmacy revenue)
- **Chronic/repeat Rx:** Growing fast online (subscription + refill reminders)
- **Acute Rx:** Still predominantly offline (patients need medicines immediately)
- **Scheduled drugs (H, H1, X):** Legally restricted online, require strict Rx verification

### Urban vs Rural
- North India: 30% of e-pharmacy market (highest)
- Rural/semi-urban: Still rely on local chemists

## 3. Dark Stores & Quick Commerce

### Quick Commerce Infrastructure (2025)
- **Blinkit:** ~1,000 dark stores, targeting 2,000 by FY26; >50% market share
- **Zepto:** ~1,100 dark stores, targeting 1,500 by mid-2025
- Total sector: 5,000–5,500 dark stores expected by FY26
- Quick commerce GMV: $6–7 Bn (2024), growing ~40% annually

### Pharmacy in Quick Commerce
- **Zepto Pharmacy:** 10-minute medicine delivery in Mumbai, Bangalore, Delhi NCR, Hyderabad
- **Blinkit:** Piloting prescription delivery in Bengaluru
- **Swiggy Instamart:** Partnered with PharmEasy for procurement + Rx verification
- **Medicine AOV:** INR 1,000+ per order vs INR 600 for groceries — higher-value category

### Regulatory Barriers
- Only licensed pharmacies can sell/dispense medicines
- AIOCD opposes quick commerce selling scheduled drugs without proper verification
- No legal framework for online medicine sale — Draft E-Pharmacy Rules still unenacted since 2018

## 4. Last-Mile Delivery

### Delivery Time Benchmarks

| Tier | Time | Players |
|---|---|---|
| Ultra-fast | 10–19 min | Zepto (10 min), Apollo 24/7 (19 min), Blinkit |
| Express | 30–60 min | Medstown, PharmEasy Express |
| Same-day | 2–6 hours | Tata 1mg, PharmEasy, Netmeds |
| Next-day | 12–24 hours | All platforms (Tier 2/3 cities) |

### Delivery Economics
- Last-mile cost: **INR 40–60 per order** (~40–60% of total delivery cost)
- If pick+pack+deliver = INR 80, and gross margin on INR 450 basket = INR 70 → **platform loses money**
- Medicine AOV advantage: INR 1,000+ vs INR 600 for groceries

### Fleet Models
- **Own fleet:** Apollo (leveraging 7,000 stores as fulfillment points)
- **Gig workers:** PharmEasy, Tata 1mg use third-party delivery
- **Store-to-door:** MedPlus and Wellness Forever fulfill from nearest store

## 5. Click-and-Collect (BOPIS)

- **Apollo:** 7,000 stores — order online, pick up from nearest outlet
- **MedPlus:** "India's Largest Retail Pharmacy Omni Channel" — store + online + call center
- **Wellness Forever:** App, website, call center, WhatsApp ordering; 400+ stores support pickup

### Why It Works for Pharmacy
- Prescription verification easier in-store (pharmacist present)
- No delivery fee
- Immediacy (no waiting)
- Trust factor (Indian consumers prefer seeing medicines before purchase)

## 6. Digital Prescription (e-Rx) & Regulation

### Current State
- **No dedicated e-pharmacy law exists** — governed by Drugs & Cosmetics Act (1940)
- **Draft E-Pharmacy Rules (2018):** Proposed but **never enacted in 7+ years**
- Delhi High Court (Nov 2023) directed government to frame policy within 8 weeks — still pending
- Same prescription can be uploaded across multiple platforms — no authenticity verification

### Telemedicine Integration
- Apollo 24/7: End-to-end (consult → digital Rx → order → delivery)
- Post-COVID, teleconsultations feed directly into pharmacy orders

## 7. Discount Economics

### Discount Levels
- Standard online: **15–20% off MRP**
- Aggressive (Truemeds): up to **51%** via generic substitution
- First-order coupons common

### Unit Economics

| Component | Offline | Online |
|---|---|---|
| Retail margin | 15–26% | Similar procurement |
| Discount to customer | 0–5% | 15–25% |
| Net margin after discount | 15–20% | Near-zero or negative |
| Delivery cost | N/A | INR 40–80 |

**Sustainability verdict:** Discounts must come down. Generic substitution (Truemeds, Medkart) is the viable path to profitability.

## 8. Omnichannel Integration

### Best-in-Class: Apollo Pharmacy
- 7,000 stores + Apollo 24/7 digital platform
- Integrated: consult → Rx → order → deliver from nearest store
- 19-minute delivery; 1M+ orders/day across channels
- "80% of Indians live within 40 minutes of an Apollo Pharmacy"

### Wellness Forever
- ZEDC (Zero Error Distribution Center) + central planogramming + POS + ERP + OMS
- Partnered with Blue Yonder for digital category management
- 4 channels: app, website, call center, WhatsApp

### Technology Stack
- Cloud-based pharmacy software: centralized stock, uniform pricing
- Unified customer profiles with purchase history + loyalty
- Real-time inventory sync across branches
- Batch tracking and expiry management across channels

## 9. WhatsApp Commerce

### Adoption
- Most-used messaging platform in India; highest open rates
- **Apollo 24/7:** WhatsApp To Order service — send prescription photo → pharmacy processes → delivery
- **Wellness Forever:** WhatsApp as primary ordering channel
- **EvitalRx:** Built-in WhatsApp integration for refill reminders

### Why It Works for Pharmacy
- Low friction for non-tech-savvy users (especially elderly)
- Prescription upload = sharing a photo
- Particularly effective in Tier 2/3 cities where app downloads are lower

---

## 10. Quick Commerce Threat

### Why Quick Commerce Wants Pharmacy
- Medicine AOV > INR 1,000 (vs grocery INR 600)
- High repeat rate (chronic patients = recurring revenue)
- Builds "super app" ecosystem
- Better gross margins than groceries

### Impact on Pharmacy Chains
- Direct competition in OTC segment
- Less threat in Rx due to regulatory complexity
- Chains responding: Apollo launched 19-min delivery, loyalty programs, subscription models
